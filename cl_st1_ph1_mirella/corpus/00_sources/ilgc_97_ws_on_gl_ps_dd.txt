Examples of inclusive language

The following are some ways in which we can adopt inclusive language to respectfully reflect different identities.

Regarding gender:

Use plural forms that include all genders when referring to mixed groups. For example, instead of “the policemen”, we can say “the police officers”; and instead of “the salesmen”, we can say “salespeople”.
Also when we refer to mixed groups, we can use both masculine and feminine nouns, such as “the boys and girls” or “the men and women”.
To give visibility to sexual diversity in a non-stereotypical or euphemistic way:

Say “sexual orientation” instead of other expressions such as “sexual preference”.
Say “gay” or “lesbian” instead of “homosexual”.
Use the pronouns with which people identify their gender, as it is a way of showing respect for their gender identity. To do this, we can ask the person in question which pronoun they identify with.
To refer to a person's different abilities: refer to the person rather than the condition. For example, refer to them as a “person with autism” instead of calling them “autistic”.
Use specific and respectful terms. For example, instead of saying the person “suffers from” a certain condition, say they are a “person with” a specific condition.
To refer to ethnic diversity:

Avoid generalizations and stereotypes.
Use terms that accurately reflect the person's cultural identity and, above all, that are their preference and with which they feel identified.
Avoid obsolete or pejorative terms such as “oriental”, which can be substituted with “Asian person” or a more specific description.
