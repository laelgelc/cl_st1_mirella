Terminology to consider using — Equity-deserving: "Those who feel or are made to feel marginalized, or that they do not belong, deserve equity as a right. They should not be given the burden of seeking it and they should not be made to feel that they get it as a privilege from the generosity of those who have the power to give it, and hence the power to take it back."

Terminology to consider avoiding — Equity-seeking.

Terminology to consider using — "...are underrepresented in...", "...have been minoritized in...", "...are marginalized from...", Historically marginalized, Historically oppressed. These terms demonstrate that exclusion is not a permanent fixture of one's identity. For example: "X are underrepresented in the literature on mental illness and youth"; "Y have been minoritized in training despite comprising the majority of the paediatric population being served"; "Z are marginalized in pregnancy care."

Terminology to consider avoiding — Underrepresented minority, Hard-to-reach populations. These terms imply that members of these groups will not achieve equal representation or access, deny groups the right to name themselves, and ignore the differences in identity and circumstances of members in the groups. They also do not recognize the ongoing marginalization and oppression experienced by communities.

Terminology to consider using — Key populations, Priority populations, At-risk populations. "It's important that our language reflects recognition of the role of social determinants of health (SDOH) in shaping population health and well-being, while at the same time acknowledging the strength, resiliency, and individuality of individuals within populations."

Terminology to consider avoiding — Vulnerable populations. This term focuses on weakness instead of action and empowerment, and implies that all individuals within a population are equally vulnerable.

Terminology to consider using and the corresponding terms to consider avoiding:

Use [name] or Dr. or Mx — avoid Mr., Mrs., Miss., Ms., Sir, or Madam (when preference is unknown).

Use Spouse or partner or significant other — avoid Husband, wife, boyfriend, girlfriend (when preference is unknown).

Use Parents, co-parent, guardians, family — avoid Mother, father, mom, dad (when preference is unknown).

Use Children or child — avoid Son, daughter (when preference is unknown).

Use Sibling — avoid Brother, sister (when preference is unknown).

Use Grandparents — avoid Grandmother, grandfather (when preference is unknown).

Use Biological parent or chosen family — avoid language that may overlook family diversity.

Use Blended family — avoid Divorced family, mixed family, stepfamily.

Use Intended parents — avoid Legal parents.

Use Gestational carrier or gestational surrogate — avoid Surrogate mother.

Use Non-gestational parent(s), non-genetic parent(s), adoptive parent(s) — avoid Adopting mom(s) or dad(s).
