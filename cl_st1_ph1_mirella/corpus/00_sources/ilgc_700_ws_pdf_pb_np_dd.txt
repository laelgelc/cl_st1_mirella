Forms of address

Although most nouns and adjectives in English are gender neutrals, there are still some gendered forms of address that are now obsolete. For instance, some nouns ending in -man can be replaced by their gender-neutral forms, such as (police officer for policeman/policewoman, spokesperson for spokesman, chair/chairperson for chairman).

These are some examples of gendered and gender-neutral forms.

   Gendered: Ladies and gentlemen (to open a speech)
   Gender-neutral: Good morning/hello everybody
   Gender-neutral: Dear colleagues/participants/audience members

   Gendered: Dear Mr. Smith
   Gender-neutral: Dear Mx. Smith
   Gender-neutral: Dear R. Smith (name/name initial and surname)

   Gendered: I talked to the girl at the door
   Gender-neutral: I talked to the young person at the door

   Gendered: The speaker can use a visual presentation if he/she wants to (he/she "inclusive" form)
   Gender-neutral: The speaker can use a visual presentation if they want to

   Gendered: The UN spokesman
   Gender-neutral: The UN spokesperson
