
HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

This guide has been created to help explain why and how some language can be inclusive, while other terms or approaches are not. To do this, we have included recommended language and terminology as well as examples of stereotypes to avoid. However, it is not an exhaustive list of 'good' and 'bad' language, nor does it cover all situations.

Language is fluid and evolves over time – meanings and connotations of words can shift quickly, and some terms may still be subject to debate. Therefore, it's important to view this information as a reference and rely on the key principles of inclusive language when uncertain. As such, CA ANZ commits to regularly reviewing this guide to ensure its relevance, sensitivity to changes and by making amendments as required.

A note on intersectionality
We offer this inclusive language guidance with reference to groups of people who may have experienced higher instances of exclusion. Identity is complex and multi-dimensional – you may have heard of the term intersectionality: the manner in which different aspects of a person's identity overlap. Intersectionality explains how people may experience overlapping forms of discrimination or exclusion based on their personal attributes. Thus, as you consider the inclusive language suggested in this guide, remember that no group or identity exists in isolation.

CARE STATEMENT

This document contains examples of non-inclusive language sometimes used in reference to under-represented groups, alongside appropriate and inclusive suggestions. This non-inclusive or negative language is outdated, offensive and may be distressing for some. It exists in this document only as an example of language to avoid, with appropriate alternatives provided.

If you find any language or concepts in this guide to be distressing, you can access member support services, including access to CA ANZ Employee Assistance Program (EAP) for all members in Australia, New Zealand and internationally.
