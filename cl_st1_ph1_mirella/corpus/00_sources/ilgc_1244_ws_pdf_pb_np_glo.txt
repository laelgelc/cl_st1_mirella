The US Office of Management and Budget currently outlines five racial groups:

White: A person having origins in any of the original peoples of Europe, the Middle East, or North Africa.

Black or African American: A person having origins in any of the Black racial groups of Africa.

American Indian or Alaskan Native: A person having origins in any of the original peoples of North and South America (including Central America) and who maintains tribal affiliation or community attachment.

Asian: A person having origins in any of the original peoples of the Far East, Southeast Asia, or the Indian subcontinent including, for example, Cambodia, China, India, Japan, Korea, Malaysia,
Pakistan, the Philippine Islands, Thailand, and Vietnam.

Native Hawaiian or other Pacific Islander: A person having origins in any of the original peoples of Hawaii, Guan, Samoa, or other Pacific Islands.

People may identify with their race, ethnicity, or both, and it's important to respect their self-identification and preferred terminology.

Sex and gender are often used interchangeably, but
they refer to different things. Sex is a classification
that traditionally refers to the biological attributes
that define someone as male, female, or intersex
(born with both male and female chromosomes
and/or sex characteristics). Gender, on the other
hand, is a social construct representing a spectrum
of identities and behaviors associated with
masculinity and femininity.

While gender identity is about who you are, gender expression is about how you show who you are.
Gender expression refers to the external display of one's gender through a combination of dress,
demeanor, social behavior, and other factors. It is often described in terms of masculinity, femininity, androgyny, or a mix.

Sexuality is a multi-faceted concept that
encompasses several aspects: sexual orientation
(a person's enduring attraction to others based
on gender), sexual behavior, sexual identity (how
an individual perceives and understands their
own sexual orientation), and sexual values and
beliefs. It also encompasses physical and
emotional attraction.

Potential Groupings:
LGBTQIA+ people (currently most inclusive; the terms that make up the abbreviation are
lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer/questioning, intersex, and asexual)
Queer (often used as an umbrella term for the LGBTQIA+ community)
Usage Examples

Sample Identifying Terms
Lesbian: women who are attracted to other women
Gay: most often refers to men who are attracted to other men
Bisexual: people who are attracted to both men and women
Transgender or trans: someone whose gender identity does not match the sex registered for
them at birth
Queer: an umbrella term that can refer to just about anyone in the community
Questioning: someone who is still exploring their gender or sexual identity
Intersex: individuals born with both male and female chromosomes and/or sex characteristics
Asexual: people who feel no sexual attraction at all
Pansexual: someone who is attracted to people of any gender or regardless of their gender
identity
Straight/heterosexual: someone who is attracted exclusively to people of the opposite sex
Cisgender: a person whose gender identity corresponds to the sex registered for them at
birth
Nonbinary: an umbrella term for gender identities that are not exclusively male or female
