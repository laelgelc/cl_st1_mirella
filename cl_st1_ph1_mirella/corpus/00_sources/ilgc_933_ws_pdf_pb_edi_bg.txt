Fostering Belonging Beyond the Health Office Through Inclusive Language

A sense of belonging in school is fostered when students feel included. School nurses can establish an inclusive school atmosphere where students feel safe, supported, accepted, and valued. The language choices of school nurses play a key role in fostering such an environment. Through consciously using language that promotes respect, acceptance, justice, and equity, nurses can support student well-being. Nurses may be using embedded terminologies and outdated language that promotes exclusion but can learn and model inclusive language practices such as cultural humility, calling in, and respecting pronouns. Making intentional choices to embrace and employ these practices is imperative to supporting student health today.

Students need to feel safe, supported, accepted, and valued. Through their daily interactions with students and caregivers (e.g., teaching staff, administrators, and clinicians), school nurses are vital in establishing a trusted, welcoming, secure, and inclusive school environment. The practice of utilizing inclusive language entails deliberately choosing words that foster an environment of belonging, respect, acceptance, and equality. The language that nurses use day to day may include embedded terminologies and dated terms that are inaccurate and not inclusive . It is important to correct any intentional or unintentional use of language that communicates exclusion or that is antithetical to fostering a sense of belonging.

The concept of belonging was first identified by Maslow (1943) while describing the Hierarchy of Needs, a model that provides insight into the understanding of human behavior. Belonging is the third tier in Maslow's (1943) Hierarchy of Needs, after physiological needs and safety and security. The desire to belong is a fundamental need that is intrinsic to all people and cultures. Feeling accepted and valued are essential human needs that unite all people . In fact, students are more motivated to learn when they feel a sense of acceptance and belonging.

Using inclusive language is not just a gesture of kindness and caring, it is essential to school health. When students and caregivers feel a sense of acceptance from the school nurse and belonging in the school community, they may be more inclined to seek and receive care, as well as attend school, and enjoy academic and social opportunities. School nurses play a pivotal role in cultivating a culture of inclusivity throughout the school. The use of inclusive language is crucial for nurturing a sense of inclusiveness, fostering a strong sense of belonging, and validating the worth of students. Alternatively, when inclusive language is not used, there is a negative impact on learning in the classroom and socialization among students.

This article highlights the importance of using inclusive language in school nursing practice and offers suggestions for school nurses to employ as they work to facilitate an environment of belonging. School nurses can champion a welcoming environment for all students and staff when consciously and purposefully choosing language that fosters acceptance and respect.


Reflexivity Statement

This article was written by two nurses whose backgrounds have afforded them many privileges, resulting in them not having personally undergone the lived experiences of the individuals under discussion in this article. While both authors are dedicated to upholding principles of justice, equity, diversity, and inclusion, it is important to note that their backgrounds do not originate from historically and intentionally excluded communities, especially in nursing where most nurses look like and identify similarly to them. Both authors have practiced self-reflection, reflected upon their positionality, and encourage readers to "call them in" if any aspect of this article is not in alignment with inclusivity or belonging.
