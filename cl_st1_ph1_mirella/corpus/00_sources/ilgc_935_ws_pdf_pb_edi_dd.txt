Calling in conversations

Exclusive statement | Sample response

"Their parents are here illegally."
"I heard you say that their parents are living here illegally. The term illegal is degrading, and no human is illegal. An appropriate and inclusive term for illegal is undocumented."

"This student is non-compliant with their medications."
"I heard you say that the student is non-compliant with their medications. Using the term non-compliant places blame on the person. I would suggest identifying why the student is not taking their medications. For example, the student could not be taking their medication because it makes them sick, or they have been unable to get to the pharmacy."

"Their diabetes is uncontrolled."
"I heard you say that their diabetes is uncontrolled. An inclusive way to describe this is using the term not managed. It is also important to describe why their diabetes is not managed well. For example, the student may need more education about carbohydrate counting or how often to take their blood sugar."

"They are a frequent flyer to the hospital."
"I heard you say that they are a frequent flyer to the hospital. An inclusive term to use is an increased risk for admission. It is also important to identify why they are at an increased risk for readmission."

"They are complaining of 10/10 pain."
"I heard you say that they were complaining of pain. The word complain has a negative association. An inclusive way to describe their pain is saying that they are reporting 10/10 pain."

Condition and pronoun discussions

Query | Sample question
Asking a student or caregiver how they prefer to call their condition.
"I want to make sure that I am being respectful. People often refer to their condition in different ways. How do you refer to your condition?"

Asking a student or caregiver what their gender identity is.
"I want to be respectful and learn more about you. What is your gender identity?"
