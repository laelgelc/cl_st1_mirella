Sistergirl (or Sistagirl): a culturally specific term describing an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person who has the spirit of a woman and takes on roles associated with women within the community, including looking after children and family.
Brotherboy (or Brothaboy): a culturally specific term describing an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person who has the spirit of a man and takes on roles associated with men within the community.

Fa'afafine and Fa'afatama: two of the four recognised genders on the island of Samoa which describe gender roles that move between the worlds of men and women.
Fakaleitī: a term used in Tonga to describe men who live their lives as women in roles, relationships and sexuality. Fakaleitī do not see themselves as trans or gay; they consider their relationships with men to be heterosexual.

Hijra: a recognised third gender used in South Asia, particularly in India, which refers to a diverse community including trans women and individuals who do not identify as either men or women.

Sex — A person's sex is based upon their sex characteristics, such as their chromosomes, hormones, and reproductive organs. Depending on individual State and Territory legislation, a legal record of a person's sex may, or may not align with their sex characteristics, and a person's sex may be recorded differently on different documents.

Gender — Gender is a social and cultural concept that refers to the way a person lives in and interacts with the world. It is about social and cultural differences in identity, expression and experience. There is a great deal of variance among societies and cultures in how different genders are understood and defined.
Gender experience describes a person's alignment with the sex recorded for them at birth.

Cis(gender) refers to a person who has reported that their gender is the same as their sex recorded at birth.

Trans(gender) refers to a person who has reported that their gender is different to their sex recorded at birth.
Agender refers to a person who does not identify with any gender. People who are agender may feel that they have no gender or that their gender is neutral.

These terms are best used as adjectives, i.e. 'a cisgender / transgender / non-binary person' NOT 'a transgender / non-binary'. While being agender may fall under the transgender umbrella, not all agender individuals necessarily identify as trans.

Variations in sex characteristics — Sex characteristics are physical traits including chromosomes, genitals, hormones, reproductive anatomy, and secondary features that develop at puberty. Not everyone is born with sex characteristics that fit medical norms for female or male bodies. Variations in sex characteristics can be determined at birth, during adolescence or later in life. People born with innate variations in sex characteristics are sometimes also called intersex.

Sexual orientation — A person's sexual orientation includes their sexual identity (how they think of their sexuality and the terms they identify with), attraction (romantic or sexual interest in another person) and sexual behaviour.
