Inclusive Language Guide

Introduction
As an organisation that values diversity and inclusion, we want everyone to feel comfortable
whether they live or work at Anchor. This guidance has been produced with our colleague
network groups, involved resident groups, and subject matter experts to challenge outdated
language and make Anchor a more welcoming and inclusive place for everyone.
Language is constantly changing and evolving, and the words we use can hold great power.
It plays a part in either reinforcing or disrupting stereotypes, and we can use it to support
and include everyone. We also wish to communicate with people in ways that respect their
identity and without reinforcing outdated or negative perceptions or stereotypes.
This guidance provides some things to think about in our language choices and, where
relevant, words and phrases to avoid. As such, language that may be considered
discriminatory is shown here, so please take care in the reading of this guidance and
prioritise your wellbeing. On the other hand, the process of changing the language we use
can be uncomfortable and may feel awkward at first. Embracing this discomfort is necessary
in order to move forward for the better.
Everyone has different individual personal preferences about language and identity. Where
possible, you should always be led by the individual you are talking to. If you’re not sure, it’s
okay to ask respectfully, and listen to the answer.
Given the ever-changing nature of language, this is not a comprehensive guide and will be
updated to reflect changes in society.

This guide was produced with input and feedback from Anchor’s colleague networks and
residents, seeking to be created by those communities referenced here. Where this was
not possible, best practice was sought from the above sources.
