5 Principles of Inclusive Language

Here are five key principles to guide your use of inclusive language in everyday interactions:

1. Avoid assumptions about identity: Don't assume an individual's gender, race, or sexual orientation.

2. If you don't know, ask: Whenever possible, ask for a person's pronouns, and use inclusive, neutral terms if you are unsure about specific parts of a person's identity such as "they" rather than "he" or "she".

3. Avoid words with negative connotations: Some terms carry inherently negative meanings. 

4. Steer clear of ableist language: Avoid using words like "blind", "OCD", "deaf", or "ADHD" as descriptors for everyday situations and behaviors.

5. Keep inclusivity in mind: Terms and identities are influenced by the intersection of experience, background, and ability (e.g., race, gender, ability). Make sure to tailor language.
