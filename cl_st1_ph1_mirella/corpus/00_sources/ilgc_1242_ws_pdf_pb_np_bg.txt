Introduction

Language shapes every human interaction, and directly reflects our values individually and as a society.
For this reason, it is critical that organizations striving for inclusivity understand and adopt language
that respects and acknowledges the full spectrum of group and individual identities.

For today's workplace, this is especially important. Increasingly, organizational culture is assessed by
customers, investors, and job candidates based on how it values and acknowledges individual and
group identities.

Diversity and Inclusion at Beacon

Comprised of business executives and civic leaders, the Beacon membership works to stay current on
business trends and leadership best practices. The Beacon Board of Directors also realizes the necessity
of a diverse and inclusive membership for any organization (or company) interested in maintaining a
competitive edge in promoting or selling their company, service, or product—including attracting and
retaining the best talent or, in Beacon's case, providing a welcoming culture for all members.

Beacon's Committee on Diversity and Inclusion

To reflect Beacon's commitment to Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion, the Board of Directors has
established the Committee on Diversity and Inclusion. The Committee initiates and facilitates
opportunities for dialogue, reflection, and learning. We educate membership on the business value, profitability, and workplace importance of diversity and inclusion, as we explore and reinforce collaborative partnerships across diverse business communities.

The Diversity & Inclusion Committee leads Beacon's commitment to advance membership awareness
and to nurture diversity. We believe that inclusivity is essential to building high-performing
organizations, and that networking is at its best when it is with a richly diverse membership in a dynamically inclusive organization.

For more information, visit us online at the Beacon Diversity & Inclusion Committee Home Page.

Purpose of this Guide

This guide provides an overview of inclusive language considerations and suggests best practices for organizations that value diversity, equity, and inclusion. It seeks to clarify confusion around terms and pronouns and ensure consistency (and a degree of sensitivity) when describing individual and group identities.

Given the evolving nature of this language, usage and related best practices may shift. These guidelines represent an approach that is as inclusive and as sensitive to individual identities as possible.

This Guide aims to provide insight into using language that is respectful, accurate, and  promotesequality and accessibility for all.
