Global sustainable development terms

The language in this field is constantly evolving and people hold differing opinions about which words work. But it is clear that some words do not work, and some do.

Problematic Phrases: Aid, Foreign Aid
Ideal Terminology: Global redistribution
Alternatives: Social development finance, international SDG finance, official development assistance (ODA)
Note: Paternalistic language implies an unequal power relationship marked by altruism which denies the unjust distribution of resources that defines that dynamic.

Problematic Phrases: Beneficiaries, world's poorest, poor people, most vulnerable people, people in need, recipients
Alternatives: People, people living in/with (extreme) poverty, communities we work with, stakeholders, partners (if actually partners), marginalised communities
Note: These phrases deny agency insofar as they define people by their poverty.

Problematic Phrases: Localisation
Ideal Terminology: Shifting power and resources to communities
Alternatives: Locally led (this involves local entities—not local offices—taking a leading role in priority setting, design, partnership formation, implementation, and defining and measuring results of projects)
Note: This phrase is often used to virtue signal minor adjustments to policies, not genuine shifts of power and resources.

Problematic Phrases: Developing/developed world, first/third world, the West
Ideal Terminology: Majority world
Alternatives: In (insert name of place), Global North and Global South, low-and-middle-income (if contextually appropriate)
Note: These terms are hierarchical and refer to exploitative relationships and the Cold War.

Problematic Phrases: In the field, on the ground
Alternatives: In (insert place name), residents of (insert place name) or more generally: in communities
Note: Historically these are colonial and militaristic terms, they also suggest that power lies elsewhere.

Problematic Phrases: Working for, helping, giving
Alternatives: Working with, the communities we work alongside, standing in solidarity with (if we are actually acting in solidarity with a group)
Note: These phrases deny agency by suggesting passive recipients of the global north's labour.

Problematic Phrases: Capacity building, empowering, knowledge transfer
Alternatives: Sharing learning and knowledge, community organising, knowledge exchange
Note: Suggests those in the minority world are givers of power, skill, and knowledge.

Problematic Phrases: Global warming, global heating, climate change
Ideal Terminology: Climate emergency
Alternatives: Climate crisis
Note: Warming and change suggest the problem is climate/atmosphere related, not the emergent global crisis it is.

Be mindful of using unnecessarily gendered phrases, for instance:

Problematic Phrases: Manpower, man hours
Alternatives: Workforce, staff/staffing, efficiency

Problematic Phrases: Girls, guys
Alternatives: Colleagues, everyone

Problematic Phrases: Mother, Father
Alternatives: Parent, guardian

Problematic Phrases: Chairman, Spokeswoman
Alternatives: Chairperson, chair, spokesperson
