QUICK LANGUAGE SUBSTITUTIONS

Item 1:
- Less inclusive: Guys (or women) when referring to people overall
- More inclusive: Folks, people, you all, y'all, teammates

Item 2:
- Less inclusive: Girls (when referring to adults)
- More inclusive: Women

Item 3:
- Less inclusive: Manpower, man hours
- More inclusive: Workforce, personnel, workers, team

Item 4:
- Less inclusive: Chairman, foreman
- More inclusive: Chairperson, chair, moderator, discussion leader

Item 5:
- Less inclusive: Wives, husbands, boyfriends, girlfriends
- More inclusive: Spouses/partners

Item 6:
- Less inclusive: Mothering, fathering
- More inclusive: Parenting

Item 7:
- Less inclusive: Normal (there's bias inherent in using one group as a standard against which others are judged. Use of the word normal as a comparison group can stigmatize people who are different and imply they are abnormal.)
- More inclusive: Typical

Item 8:
- Less inclusive: Minorities (not all marginalized groups are minorities, and a broader term is generally inclusive of more than race and gender)
- More inclusive: Marginalized groups or underrepresented groups

PHRASES IT MIGHT BE BETTER TO AVOID


1. Girl/Girls: For anyone over 18 years old, woman or women is a better choice.

2. Guys: When referring to mixed-gender groups. Alternatives: people, folks, teammates.

3. Females: To many English speakers, "females" sounds like a scientific designation one would use for animals or plants. Try "women" instead.

4. Hacker: In job descriptions, the term "hacker" can be tough for many to identify with. Alternatives: engineer, developer.

5. Handicap: Some disability advocates believe this term is rooted in a correlation between a disabled individual and a beggar, "who had to beg with a cap in his or her hand because of the inability to maintain employment." Better alternative: Disabled.

6. Housekeeping: In reference to office work, this language can feel gendered. Suggested alternative: maintenance, cleanup.

7. Ladies/gals: Terms like "ladies", "gals" or others can feel patronizing to some. Try women instead, or "folks" or "people" for mixed-gender groups.

8. Man: As a synonym for work - as in "man hours," "man the inbox," "man the conference booth" - this is unnecessarily gendered language. Try using work instead.

9. Master/slave: Problematic term sometimes used to refer to one machine that has the original copy of data and others that automatically update themselves to match its data. Replacements include primary/replica, primary/standby.

10. Mom test/girlfriend test: A sexist term for putting a product in front of unfamiliar visitors to learn more about how they would use it. The assumption that if such a person can use a program, anyone can, is both sexist and ageist. Alternatives: user testing.
