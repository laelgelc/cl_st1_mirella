Anatomical and Reproductive Care Terms — specific examples of more inclusive language:

Instead of "Male penis/Female vagina," use "Penis/Vagina." Instead of "Male prostate/Female uterus," use "Prostate/Uterus." Instead of "Male and Female condoms," use "External and Internal condoms." Instead of "Female's menstrual cycle," use "Menstrual cycle." Instead of "Feminine products," use "Sanitary napkins and tampons." Instead of "Pregnant woman," use "Pregnant person." Instead of "Maternity/Paternity leave," use "Parental leave." Instead of "Natural/Real parent," use "Biological/Birth parent." Instead of "Mother/Father," use "Parent/Guardian." Instead of "Sister/Brother," use "Sibling." Instead of "Grandmother/Grandfather," use "Grandparent." Instead of "Son/Daughter/Kids," use "Child/Teenager/Children." Instead of "Wife/Husband," use "Spouse/Partner/Significant Other."

Work Place Terms and Slang: Occupational and societal roles can be filled by peoples regardless of their sexual identity, gender, ethnicity, etc. The following terms are suggested:

Instead of "Mankind," use "Humankind/Human beings." Instead of "Fireman/Policeman/Postman," use "Fire Fighter/Police Officer/Postal Worker." Instead of "Man up," use "Be brave/strong/resilient." Instead of "Manpower," use "Working hours." Instead of "Manmade," use "Handmade." Instead of "Guys," use "Everyone/Classmates/Students/Lab partners/Co-workers/Friends." Instead of "Ladies/Gentlemen," use "People/Colleagues/Individuals/Attendees/Clientele/Staff/Customers." Instead of "Lowest man on the totem pole," use a person's name and actual job title — this expression is outdated and is considered offensive, especially to Indigenous peoples. Instead of "Boss man," use the person's name or title in a respectful manner. Instead of "Hello guys, girls, gals," use "Hello everyone/class/students/team/audience members/folks/y'all" (note: y'all is a colloquial expression more often heard and accepted in the USA rather than in Canada).

Suggested terms to offset ageism:

Instead of "Elderly, Aged, Seniors, Senior citizens, Old people, Geezer, Granny, Codger," use "Older adults, Older individuals, Older population." Instead of "Senile/demented elderly person," use "Older person with dementia." Instead of "Sweet/adorable/cute old lady," describe the individual specifically and accurately rather than using these adjectives which are most often used to describe babies. Better to replace with a specific description such as "Jessie is the most caring/generous/thoughtful/friendly person you'll ever meet," etc. (if it is true). Instead of "Weak/incapacitated/feeble/poor old man," be specific and accurate: "Joe requires some assistance with heavy-lifting/yard-work/cooking/driving/toileting." Instead of "Kids/children" (when the group of people are no longer children), use terms such as "students/class." If the age group is known to be exclusively teenagers, use "Teens/Young adults." Instead of "Silver tsunami/gray wave," use "Increased number in older adults." Instead of "Young at heart," note that this phrase implies only young people can enjoy life, fall in love, have fun, etc. Instead of "You can't teach an old dog new tricks," note that this expression is outdated and is considered offensive, in that it implies older people are not capable of learning new skills or new information. Instead of "Old maid, Spinster, Coot, Curmudgeon," note that these terms are disrespectful — just avoid. Instead of "Adult diaper," note that "diaper" is a word associated with babies — it is better to use the product's brand name, or just "briefs," "panties," or "underpants."


Avoid terms such as "overcome a disability" and "suffering with." Suggested terms to replace outdated disability language:

Instead of "Cripple/Handicapped/Invalid," use "A person with a physical disability." Instead of "Wheelchair bound," use "Wheelchair user" (wheelchair-bound is typically not used as it makes it sound like someone is stuck in a wheelchair). Instead of "Disabled employees," use "Employees that are disabled." Instead of "Dog for disabled people," use "Guide dog, Service dog." Instead of "Brain damaged," use "Person with traumatic brain injury." Instead of "Epileptic," use "A person with epilepsy." Instead of "Demented/senile/afflicted," use "A person with dementia." Instead of "Special Needs," use "A person with a disability." Instead of "Developmentally delayed," use "Child with a congenital disability" or "Child with a birth impairment." Instead of "Downs/Downs child," use "Child with Down Syndrome." Instead of "Dwarf/midget," use "A person of short stature." Instead of "Birth defect/Developmentally-delayed person," use "A person with a congenital disability/A person with a developmental delay." Instead of "Mentally challenged/Mental retardation," use "A person with intellectual disabilities." Instead of "Brain defect," use "A person with a learning disability." Instead of "Learning/cognitively disabled," use "A person with a congenital cognitive disability." Instead of "Mentally ill/Nuts/Crazy/Psycho," use "A person with a mental illness/disorder," "A person living with a mental health condition," or "A person that has been having mental health challenges." Instead of "Schizophrenic," use "A person with schizophrenia." Instead of "Suicide victim," use "They died by suicide." Instead of "They committed suicide," use "They took their own life." Instead of "Unsuccessful/failed suicide," use "Attempted suicide." Instead of "Alcoholic," use "A person with alcohol use disorder." Instead of "Drug addict/Drug abuser/This person used to be an addict," use "Person with substance use disorder/This person is in recovery." Instead of "Felon/Criminal/Ex-con/Crook/Perpetrator/Gangster," use "Person with criminal record (though shouldn't be relevant to health care settings most of the time)." Instead of "OCD-behaviours/Anorexic-behaviours," avoid using diagnostic terms to describe behaviours; list the behaviours specifically including time frames, and allow for the specialist to make the final diagnosis. Instead of "Anorexic," use "A person experiencing anorexia nervosa." Instead of "Normal, healthy, able-bodied," use "A person without disabilities."


Suggested terms for race and ethnicity:

Instead of "Asian" or "Oriental," use the more specific preferred term: Asian Canadian, Asian American, South Asian, East Asian, or when possible refer to specific nation, region, or country.

Instead of "BIPOC (Black, Indigenous, and People of Colour — to be used in context)," use IBPOC (Indigenous, Black and People of Colour) — please note that the acronym IBPOC is used in BC, whereas BIPOC is used more often in the USA. When possible refer to specific groups of people.

Instead of "Minority," use "Racialized peoples" or "Member of racialized group."

Instead of "Indian," "Aboriginal People," or "Native People," note that these terms are considered outdated and can be found derogatory. These terms are in many governmental, historical, and legal documents and when used, references and context are important. Preferred terms include: Indigenous person; Indigenous peoples (in Canada, collectively includes First Nation, Métis and Inuit). Best practice includes using the Nation, or specific Name of Indigenous group (e.g. Ojibwe, Potawatomie, Odawa, Anishinaabe, Oneida, Mohawk, Haudenosaunee). There are more than 600 distinct First Nations communities in Canada.

Instead of "Eskimo," note that the term Eskimo is considered to be derogatory. Preferred Term: Inuit.

Instead of "Indigenous person or Indigenous culture (singular form)" or "First Nation People," best practice includes using the Nation, or specific Name of Indigenous group. There are more than 600 distinct First Nations communities in Canada.

Instead of "Afro-American," "Coloured," or "Negro," preferred alternatives include: Black people(s), Black communities, Black culture (always capitalize when referring to Black peoples/communities/culture and use as an adjective); African Canadian (people of African descent from Canada); African American (people of African descent from Canada); Afro-Latin American (people of African descent from Latin America); African; Black Europeans of African ancestry; Black Britons (Black British peoples); Caribbean Canadian (people of Caribbean descent from Canada). Please note that it is not appropriate to assume that a Black person is a descendent from Africa. Be sure to use a person's exact preference.

Instead of "LatinX," preferred alternatives include: Hispanic, Latin American, or best practice is to refer to the specific country: Cuban, Columbian, Venezuelan, etc.
