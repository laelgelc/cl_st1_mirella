ABORIGINAL AND TORRES STRAIT ISLANDER PEOPLES

WHO ARE ABORIGINAL AND TORRES STRAIT ISLANDER PEOPLES?

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples are people of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander descent, who identify as Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander and are accepted as an Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander person in the community in which they live or have lived. 'Aboriginal' refers to the first peoples of mainland Australia. 'Torres Strait Islander' refers to the original peoples of the Torres Strait Islands.

MAORI

WHO ARE MAORI?
Maori is a collective term for the iwi taketake (indigenous tribes) of Aotearoa New Zealand. Their histories describe the arrival of their tipuna (ancestors) in great waka (canoes) from across Te Moana-nui-a-Kiwa (The Pacific Ocean). Many Maori whakapapa (trace lineage) to specific waka and the iwi (tribes) and hapu (sub-tribes) originating from each.

These ancestral lines are important to establish kinship and relationship to the whenua (land). Maori often identify with one or more iwi, each with its own ancestors, landmarks, and history.

Moriori are a distinct group of indigenous people who eventually settled in Rekohu (The Chatham Islands).

Te Ao Maori
Te Ao Maori (the Maori World) encompasses key elements including te reo Maori (Maori language), tikanga (protocols), matauranga (knowledge), and matapono (principles).

Te reo Maori
Te reo Maori is an official language and contributes to the unique identity of Aotearoa New Zealand. It is a taonga (treasure) deserving of protection under the terms of Te Tiriti o Waitangi (The Treaty of Waitangi).

AGE
Age bias is so prevalent in our societies and workplaces that many people may not recognise it as problematic. In fact, the World Health Organisation's 2021 Global Report on ageism found that one in two people worldwide could be considered ageist, calling ageism 'prevalent, ubiquitous, and insidious'. Ageism is real and it is important that we acknowledge and address it, ensuring fairness and respect for all generations. When we think about age bias, we often think of older adults. While it is true that ageism predominantly affects older adults, age bias can create misleading and negative perceptions of individuals at any age. It's crucial that we understand these commonly upheld age-based misconceptions so that we can move beyond stereotypes and provide more nuanced representations of age.

Ageism involves stereotyping or discriminating against someone based on their age. It can be a sensitive issue to tackle, given the varying preferences people have about how they are treated or described in terms of their age. For example, some people may view referring to someone as "young" as a compliment. However, some receivers may feel the term signals negative stereotypes about inexperience and capability levels.

DISABILITY AND ACCESSIBILITY

Disability encompasses a wide range of physical, sensory, cognitive, and mental health conditions. Much of the language commonly used to describe people living with disability is negatively phrased, defines those with disability by their disability, or suggests that disability is abnormal and perpetuates stereotypes of weakness.

NEURODIVERSITY

Neurodiversity is a term coined by Australian sociologist Judy Singer in the late 1990s to describe 'the limitless variability of human cognition.' It includes people on the autism spectrum as well as those with other conditions, such as dyslexia, dyspraxia, dysgraphia and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). Even though it's not a medical condition, some people have embraced the term to describe themselves, as in 'a person with neurodiversity'. Other terms, such as neuroatypical and neurodivergent have since emerged.
