Suggested language:
* Instead of "The elderly, the old, pensioners" -> Try: "Mature individual, older individuals, senior citizens"
* Instead of "The young" -> Try: "Young person, young adults"
* Instead of "The boys/girls in the office" -> Try: "Everyone in the office, Our colleagues in the office"
* Instead of "A young and vibrant team" -> Try: "An effective and vibrant team"
* Instead of "A mature workforce" -> Try: "An experienced workforce"

* Instead of "The disabled, the handicapped" -> Try: "Person with a disability, Disabled people (use capital 'D')"
* Instead of "Normal, healthy, able-bodied person" -> Try: "Non-disabled - a person who does not have a disability."
* Instead of "Disabled toilets/lifts, Toilets/lifts for the disabled" -> Try: "Accessible toilets/lifts"
* Instead of "Wheelchair-bound, confined to a chair" -> Try: "Someone who uses a wheelchair, or wheelchair user"
* Instead of "Cancer or dementia victims" -> Try: "Person living with dementia"
* Instead of "Deaf and dumb" -> Try: "Deaf people, Hearing impaired, Blind or partially sighted people"
* Instead of "Nuts, psycho, mad" -> Try: "People living with mental health issues"
* Instead of "Retarded, slow, Schizophrenic" -> Try: "Neurodiverse/Neurodivergent - includes people who are autistic/with ADHD/with dyslexia, Person who has schizophrenia"
* Instead of "Challenged, special" -> Try: "Person with a learning disability or difficulty"
* Instead of "Courageous, inspiring, heroic" -> Try: "Successful, productive"

* Use gender neutral terms: Instead of "Man, Mankind" -> Try: "Humans, Humankind"; Instead of "Workmanship" -> Try: "Quality of work/skills"; Instead of "Man the desk/phones, Man-made" -> Try: "Attend the desk/phones, Artificial, manufactured, synthetic, plant based"
* Use gender neutral pronouns and expressions: Instead of "Welcome ladies and gentlemen, boys and girls" -> Try: "Welcome to friends and colleagues, Welcome to everyone"
* Use person-centred language: Instead of "The transgender" -> Try: "Trans people"
* Respect the preferences of those people who want to be referred to by gender neutral pronouns: Instead of "She, her, hers and he, him, his" -> Try: "They, them, theirs (e.g. Xena ate their food because they were hungry.) It is correct to use in the singular."
* Use terms that include all relationships: Instead of "Boyfriend, wife, husband" -> Try: "Partner, spouse"
* Official forms should include a comprehensive list of titles, sorted alphabetically rather than following any perceived hierarchy: Instead of "Prof, Dr, Mr, Mrs, Miss, Ms, Mx, etc" -> Try: "Dr, Miss, Mr, Mrs, Ms, Mx, Prof, etc [blank]"
* Avoid titles that imply the usual job-holder being of a particular gender: Instead of "Cleaning ladies, Policeman" -> Try: "Cleaners, Police officer"
* Be mindful of appropriate and respectful in-group versus out-group naming, i.e, avoid using terms that are only used by individuals that self-identify as part of a specific community: Instead of "Queer (only use if you identify as queer), Agender (only use if you identify as agender)" -> Try: "Queer communities, N/A"
* Avoid using patronising terms that may cause offence to a particular gender: Instead of "Girls, Ladies, Dear, Son, Love" -> Try: "The person's name, their professional title or, friends and colleagues."
* Avoid irrelevant gender descriptions: Instead of "A female scientist, A male nurse" -> Try: "A scientist, A nurse"

* Avoid terms that suggest a degree of voluntary choice when this is not necessarily the case: Instead of "Sexual preference, Lifestyle choice" -> Try: "Sexual orientation"
* Use person-centred language: Instead of "Lesbians, gays, bisexuals" -> Try: "Lesbian, gay, bisexual people"
* Use language that does not assume heterosexuality as the norm: Instead of "Invite your boyfriend/husband" -> Try: "Invite your spouse/partner."; Instead of "Family planning clinic" -> Try: "Sexual health clinic or sexual health and wellbeing clinic"
* Recognise diverse family formation: Instead of "Mother and father" -> Try: "Parents, guardians, caregivers"
* Recognise and respect the difference between sexual orientation and gender identity: Don't use 'LGBTQ+' if you are only talking about sexual orientation. Don't use 'straight' as the opposite of 'LGBTQ+' (transgender people can be any sexual orientation, including straight). Only use LGBTQ+ when referring to both sexual orientation and gender identity-based communities. Use 'straight cisgendered' or 'ally'.

* Official forms should include this new legal relationship status: Instead of "What is your marital status? single, married, divorced, widow/widower" -> Try: "What is your relationship status? What is your marital/civil partnership status? single, married/civil partner, divorced/dissolved civil partnership, widow/widower/surviving civil partner"

* Use adjectives rather than nouns when it is necessary to refer to someone's race: Instead of "Half caste, mixed race" -> Try: "Biracial, multiracial, Black, black person"
* Avoid irrelevant ethnic descriptions: Instead of "The Asian doctor" -> Try: "The doctor"
* Don't use 'minority group' as it implies inferior social position and is often relative to geographic location: Instead of "Minority group" -> Try: "Minority"
* Avoid stereotyping, e.g., making positive or negative generalisations about members of a particular racial, ethnic or national group. Avoiding patronising comments based on stereotypes: Instead of "Chinese people are really good at maths, You speak such good English!" -> Try: "N/A"
* Avoid outdated terms: Instead of "BAME, BME, mixed race, coloured" -> Try: "People/person of colour, black, indigenous"

* Avoid using Christian-centric terms, not only on ground of respect but also for practical reasons. The term 'last name' should not be used as it could be confusing to Asian groups who place their family name first: Instead of "Christian name" -> Try: "First name, given name"; Instead of "Surname, Last name" -> Try: "Family name" (Surname is not unacceptable. However, this word may originate from sire-name, or the name derived from one's father.)
* Official forms should include both options for religion and belief: Instead of "What is your religion? Christian, Muslim, Hindu, Jewish, Sikh, Other" -> Try: "Do you regard yourself as belonging to any particular religion? Yes, No. Alternatively: What is your religion or belief?"
* Use legally correct terminology: Instead of "Faith, Religious discrimination" -> Try: "Religion or belief, Discrimination on grounds of religion or belief"
