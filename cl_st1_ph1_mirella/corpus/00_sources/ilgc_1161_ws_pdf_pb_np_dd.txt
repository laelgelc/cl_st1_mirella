
Preferred Terminology

Here are some common terminology do's and don'ts, again culled from the GLAAD Media Reference Guide, which explicates these preferences fully.

Do Not Use | Use Instead
homosexual (n. or adj.) | gay (adj.), gay man or lesbian (n.), gay person/people
homosexual relations/relationship, homosexual couple, homosexual sex, etc. | relationship, couple (or, if necessary, gay/lesbian/same-sex couple), sex, etc.
sexual preference | sexual orientation or orientation
gay lifestyle, homosexual lifestyle, or transgender lifestyle | LGBTQ people and their lives
transgenders, a transgender | transgender people, a transgender person
sex change, preoperative, postoperative | transition
biologically male, biologically female, genetically male, genetically female, born a man, born a woman | assigned male at birth, assigned female at birth or designated male at birth, designated female at birth

So, "A student who loses too much sleep may have trouble focusing during his or her exams" becomes, "Students who lose too much sleep may have trouble focusing during their exams."

 If the prose bears it, use the noun: "The poets gathered in the auditorium," instead of, "They gathered in the auditorium."

Instead of writing, "The mailman visited in the afternoons," you can write, "The mail was delivered in the afternoons," or "The mail carrier visited in the afternoons."

Conversely, if you are unsure of the person's specific disability, it is better to use umbrella terms; for example, if you think a person has cerebral palsy, but you are not sure, use person with a disability as opposed to person with cerebral palsy.


Do Not Use | Use Instead
the disabled, challenged, differently abled | people with disabilities*
wheelchair-bound or confined to a wheelchair | person who uses a wheelchair or wheelchair user
cripple, spastic, victim | person with a disability, person with multiple sclerosis, person with cerebral palsy
mentally handicapped | intellectually disabled
normal | nondisabled
schizo, mad, crazy | person with a mental health disability
suffers from Down syndrome, victim of Down syndrome | person with Down (or Down's) syndrome
suffers from (e.g., asthma) | has (e.g., asthma)

*Please note that some people prefer identity-first language, especially in the autistic, blind, and deaf communities.


Preferred Terminology

Do Not Use | Use Instead
disadvantaged, struggling, underprivileged | making ends meet on low wages, working hard to make ends meet, people facing barriers, people facing challenges because of poverty
poor, poorest, the poor, impoverished, low class, lower class people | people with limited means, low-wealth communities, people with incomes below the poverty line, low-income families
food stamps, the hungry | Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, people experiencing food insecurity
working poor, deserving poor | people with low incomes
welfare, welfare mothers | Families who receive Temporary Assistance for Needy Families benefits
third world countries, developing countries | use the names of the countries
inner city, projects, ghetto, high-poverty neighborhoods | under-resourced, disinvested, neighborhoods with high poverty rates, low-income neighborhoods, areas with poverty
homeless, the homeless, vagrant, homeless shelter | people without fixed, regular, or adequate housing; transitional shelter; houseless; people experiencing houselessness; the unhoused
crime-ridden | neighborhoods with high crime rates
high school dropout | people with a grade school education
achievement gap | opportunity gap
low-performing school | under-resourced school

Preferred Terminology

Do Not Use | Use Instead
religion as a catch-all term | belief, faith expression, cultural tradition
church as a catch-all term | use the correct term for a person's place of faith gathering, synagogue, mosque, etc.
religious as a catch-all term | person of faith; consider also that faith, per se, might not be part of a person's experience; consider a person may identify as having spirituality as opposed to religion, or might have no spiritual or religious belief at all, or be agnostic or atheist
God as a catch-all term | use the term used by the person you are writing about
worship as a catch-all term | be careful with words like this; consider using words like faith or spiritual practice; remember that not all practices are considered worship
