kids (in reference to post-secondary students) | students

son/daughter | children

family resemblance | if used without knowing someone's family context, this term assumes a resemblance and familial relationship where one may not exist.


Instead of... | Consider...
disabled community/disabled sport | disability community/sport for athletes with a disability
person afflicted with/suffers from/is stricken with/a victim of XXXX | person with/person who has been diagnosed with XXXX
learning disabled | person with a learning disability
special ed student/special education student | student who receives academic accommodation
able-bodied | this term implies that people with disabilities lack "able bodies" or the ability to use their bodies well; use the phrase "does not have a disability" or "non-disabled"
handicapped parking/handicapped bathrooms | accessible parking/accessible bathrooms
special needs | functional needs/accessibility requirements
the blind | people who are blind/people who are visually impaired/people who have low vision
mute/non-verbal | person who is unable to speak/person who uses a communication device/communicates without using words
caretaker | caregiver
the hearing impaired/the deaf | person who is deaf (little to no functional hearing, often communicates through sign language)/person who is hard of hearing (mild to moderate hearing loss, may or may not use sign language)
Deaf vs. deaf | capital "D" typically refers to the culture (i.e., the Deaf community); lowercase "d" generally refers to audiological status



Instead of... | Consider...
crazy/insane/psycho/mentally ill/emotionally disturbed/demented/nuts | person diagnosed with a mental health condition or mental illness/person with a psychiatric disorder
offensive slang when describing a situation, e.g., mental/crazy/psycho/nuts/looney | wild/absurd/surreal
is a paranoid schizophrenic/is anorexic/is OCD/is bipolar | has paranoid schizophrenia/has anorexia nervosa/has obsessive-compulsive disorder/has bipolar disorder
committed suicide/unsuccessful or failed suicide attempt | died by suicide/attempted suicide
clean (with respect to substance use) | abstinent
dirty | actively using/positive for substance use
former or reformed addict/alcoholic | person in recovery/person in long-term recovery
harm reduction | this term reflects a progressive approach to substance use, accepting that not all people who use substances can, or want to, stop. It acknowledges that we should work to ensure the safety and humanity of people who use substances, reducing negative consequences where possible, and making different choices accessible without judgment
addict/alcoholic/drug abuser/substance abuser/junkie/crackhead | person with a substance use disorder


Instead of... | Consider...
sexual preference/lifestyle/choice | sexual orientation
admitted homosexual/outwardly homosexual | out/out gay man/lesbian/queer person
gay community, when intending to reference 2SLGBTQIA+ community members who aren't explicitly gay | 2SLGBTQIA+ or be specific when speaking to a particular community
passing/fooling/pretending/masquerading (as straight, for example) | do not use these terms, they promote the harmful belief that individuals are deceitful in their sexual identities
homosexual | gay or lesbian, to describe people attracted to members of the same sex
gay (as a noun, e.g., he is a gay) | gay man/gay person
bisexual, when intending to reference 2SLGBTQIA+ community members attracted to people of all genders or attracted to people regardless of gender | pansexual


Instead of... | Consider...
guys, e.g., "Hi guys, how's it going" when addressing a group of people, do not assume their genders | hi folks/greetings guests/hi everyone
born or biological male, female | assigned male, female at birth (AMAB, AFAB)
transsexual/tranny | transgender/trans person
deadnaming, using the name someone used before they transitioned | use the name someone identifies with, never use their dead name. This also applies to pronouns!
opposite sex, e.g., a person of the opposite sex, this reinforces a sex and gender binary, and ignores the reality of intersex persons | of a different gender, e.g., a person of different gender
chosen or new gender | gender
sex change/gender reassignment surgery | gender-affirming surgery
gender dysphoria, psychiatric vocabulary that focuses on the distress or pain caused when assigned sex at birth does not mirror a person's gender identity | gender euphoria, focusing on gender euphoria instead of dysphoria centres the positive aspects of being trans or gender expansive
pregnant woman | pregnant person
real man/woman | cisgender, refers to an individual whose gender identity aligns with their sex assigned at birth
defining roles via gender, e.g., policeman, chairman, manning a booth | gender-neutral terms, e.g., police officer, chair or chairperson, staffing a booth
manmade | artificial/constructed/synthetic

Instead of... | Consider...
black community/culture | Black community/culture
blackmark | a negative
blacklist/blackball | ban/bar/boycott/exclude/ostracize
visible minority group/minorities | racialized person/group/communities
third-world country | developing country
slaves | enslaved people
ghetto (as an adjective) | it's difficult to disassociate the term from its use to characterize low-income Black Americans, so when "ghetto" is used as an adjective, it sounds like a racial insult
grandfather clause/grandfathered in, this term has its roots in America's racial history. It entered lexicon in the 19th century with legislation that intended to prevent Black people from voting, without denying white people the same rights | legacy

Additional communication guidelines to consider related to Indigenous peoples in Canada:

Instead of... | Consider...
indigenous people | Indigenous peoples, the "s" in peoples is critical to the plurality of Indigenous communities. The capital "I" is also imperative
Inuit people | Inuit, in the Inuktitut language, the term Inuit translates to "the people" and is therefore already pluralized
Canada's indigenous people | Indigenous peoples in Canada
tribe, pow wow, spirit animal, totem pole | do not use metaphors with specific significance to Indigenous groups to describe non-Indigenous experiences
Indian, Aboriginal (unless part of a formal or organizational name, or tied to historical government acts/documents) | Indigenous

Instead of... | Consider...
the homeless/a homeless person | people without housing/individual experiencing homelessness
at-risk, as an adjective, this is stigmatizing language and does not account for the intersectionality and complexity of peoples' lives | be specific about the systemic causes at the root of the issue and what the person is at risk for, or use quotation marks ("at-risk") to demonstrate the problematic implications of this term
poor/underprivileged/poverty-stricken/disadvantaged (to describe individuals or groups) | experiencing economic hardship
coded language like "inner city," "ghetto," and "the projects" | do not use these terms as they conflate social class with race and ethnicity
leveling the playing field | avoid using this term; game-based metaphors suggest winners and losers
white trash | do not use this term as it demeans rural populations with low income levels and infers by adding the qualifier of "white" that all other racialized groups are "trash" by default
blue or white collar/unskilled labour | do not use these terms, all labour is skilled
well-educated/less-educated, this language assumes that formal Euro-Western education is the only means to gain knowledge and ignores lived experiences | specify the degree or formal education without assigning value to it, e.g., Toni holds a Bachelor's degree
food desert, the term assumes that scarcity of food is a naturally occurring situation rather than something systemic; it ignores the communities and lives in these areas given that "desert" implies empty, desolate environments | reflect on the context and if "food security," "food apartheid," "food justice" or "food sovereignty" would be more appropriate
prostitution/prostitute | sex work/sex worker
