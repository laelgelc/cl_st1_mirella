
Instead of "boys and girls", "guys", "ladies and gentlemen" → At Grace, we say: people, folks, friends, readers, mathematicians...

Instead of, when reading a book, "the boy/girl on this page…" → At Grace, we say: child, person, character

Instead of sweetheart/honey/similar pet names → At Grace, we say: child's name or "child/friend in the blue shirt"

Instead of using a boy/girl pattern (e.g. for lining up) → At Grace, we say: group by types of shoes, alphabetical, for example

Instead of boys'/girls' colors/toys → At Grace, we say: use the name of the item or color

Instead of assuming gender based on stereotypes (hair, clothing, appearance) → At Grace, we say: respectfully ask how they identify if familiar; establishing a culture of sharing affirming pronouns in class

Outdated Terms
- Hermaphrodite — Intersex is the appropriate word for someone born with ambiguous genitalia that is indiscernably male or female
- Transexual/Transvestite/Crossdresser — outdated terms to refer to what is now incorporated under the umbrella of transgender.

Outdated Terms
- Traditional Family — we actively try to undo notions of a "typical" or "normal" family structure, each family is unique
- "Real" parents — a preferred term is birth parents

Instead of Mom and Dad → At Grace, we say: grown-ups, folks, or family

Instead of parents → At Grace, we say: grown-ups, folks, family, ...and guardians

Instead of nanny/babysitter → At Grace, we say: caregiver, guardian

Instead of is adopted → At Grace, we say: was adopted

Instead of husband, wife, boyfriend, girlfriend → At Grace, we say: spouse/partner/significant other

Outdated Terms
- Sexual Preference — a preference implies a choice in the matter, when sexual orientation is more often than not something we're born with
- Homosexual — gay, lesbian, bisexual, queer, etc. are more appropriate for individuals, and LGBTQ+ is most up to date when talking about the larger community

Instead of Diverse/minority → Say: Person of color (if how they self-identify), marginalized identity/population

Instead of "What are you? Where are you from?" → Say: What is your cultural/ethnic background? Where are your ancestors/is your family from?

Instead of "What race are you?" → Say: Ask how they (self-)identify

Instead of "They faced discrimination because of their skin color/race/etc." → Say: "They faced discrimination because of racism" (a person's identity is never the problem, it's the systems of oppression)

- Caucasian — The correct term is White. White is a more accurate description of light-skinned people of European descent.

Instead of "What religion are you?" → Say: Are any religious/faith traditions important to you?

Instead of "What did you get for (any holiday)?" / "What are you going to be for Halloween?" → Say: Do you celebrate holidays?

Instead of "Merry Christmas!/Happy Holidays!" → Say: Have a great break!

Instead of "A person is learning disabled..." → Say: A person has a learning difference, or better, a specific difference

Instead of "A person is handicapped" → Say: A person has a physical disability or is differently abled

Instead of "I am 'so OCD'" → Say: You might refer to your habits (I am so hyper-focused on organization) rather than use a mental health condition as an adjective

Instead of "Where did you go for break?" → Say: Name something you learned during break. Tell me something that happened during break.

Instead of "Everyone has (insert item)" → Say: Some people have… Not everyone has...

Instead of Assuming transportation to school → Say: How did you get to school today?

Instead of Assumptions about material things/access → Say: (Be sensitive that kids might not have what they need at home)
