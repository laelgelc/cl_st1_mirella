Avoid → Use:

This policy is for women. → This policy is for women, including LGBTIQA+ women and non-binary people to whom it is relevant. Or: In this policy we use the term women in an inclusive way, referring to everyone who sees themselves as a woman as well as non-binary people to whom this policy is relevant.

Women (without any definitions or saying 'women only') → Women, including cis and trans women as well as non-binary people to whom this is relevant. Or: Women, including non-binary people and gender diverse people who feel like this space or event fits their experiences or would be beneficial to them. Or: Women, including non-binary and gender-diverse people with lived experience of misogyny or sexism.

Men (without any definitions or saying 'men only') → Men, including cis and trans men as well as non-binary people to whom this is relevant. Or: Men, including non-binary people and/or gender-diverse people who feel that this space or event fits their experiences or would be beneficial to them. Or: Men, including non-binary and/or gender-diverse people with experiences related to toxic masculinity.

Know: Phrases like 'women and LGBTIQA+ people' can suggest a false opposition that LGBTIQA+ people cannot be women, which is not always true. Use 'women including LGBTIQA+ people' instead.

Additional terminology guidance
Use 'inclusion' instead of 'inclusivity'
Inclusivity refers to the act of not excluding. Inclusion, on the other hand, refers to the act of actively including people, and is therefore the preferred term as it signals an active, positive duty to include people.
Avoid → Use:

Inclusivity → Inclusion

Avoid → Use:

Mammograms for women / Cervical screens for women / Abortions for women / Prostate examinations for men / Testicular examinations for men → [Procedure] for all people with [body part], or [Procedure] for all those who need it

Male pattern balding → Hair loss
Female/male reproductive organs → Internal reproductive organs

Avoid → Use:

Images or illustrations that reinforce gender roles and stereotypes (for example, in the context of a flyer about the construction industry portraying only men) → Images or illustrations that represent gender diversity beyond the binary

Images or illustrations that present one 'default' representation of LGBTIQA+ people (for example, as young and able-bodied people from an Anglo-Celtic background) → Ensure visual materials reflect people across a range of races, body types, genders, and abilities within LGBTIQA+ communities

Including images or illustrations of LGBTIQA+ people only in LGBTIQA+ related or themed documents → Include images or illustrations of LGBTIQA+ people in any document or material which is designed for the general public and specific population groups (e.g. people of colour)
Representing LGBTIQA+ people only in the context of pride parades, parties, or excessive LGBTIQA+ flags use → Include images or illustrations of LGBTIQA+ people in ways that signal they are LGBTIQA+ without making it stereotypical (e.g. as a family with children, or a professional). You can achieve this by integrating LGBTIQA+ symbols in more subtle ways (e.g. as an item of clothing). You can also represent people engaging in a variety of activities.
Representing LGBTIQA+ people only with a focus on sexual practices and behaviours → Include non-romantic depictions like friendships or community gatherings to avoid perpetuating assumptions about romantic partnerships being the only meaningful relationships.
