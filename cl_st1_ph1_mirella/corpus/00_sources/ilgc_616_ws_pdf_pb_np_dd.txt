(Words changed: crazy → good; insane → fun; the disabled, the transgendered and minorities → persons with disabilities, transgender persons, and other underrepresented communities; turn a blind eye → be insensitive; blacklist → list of terms to exclude; the guys → the team.)



EXAMPLES OF PHRASES TO REPLACE

(Note: Some phrases do not directly translate from the English language and may not be culturally relevant depending on geographical location.)

Instead of: Abbreviations (ATSI, TSI, IP)
Say: Consider writing in full
Why: Abbreviations used to describe people, especially Indigenous Peoples, may cause offense and should not be used in either verbal or written communication.

Instead of: Basket Case
Say: Nervous
Why: The origin of the term: WWI veterans who lost all their limbs were sometimes referred to as 'basket cases' as a reference to the baskets they would be carried around in.

Instead of: Blacklist/Whitelist
Say: Blocklist/Allowlist
Why: Though not intentionally discriminatory and not directly race-related, the phrases can have racial connotations that associate 'black' with negative emotions and 'white' with positive emotions.

Instead of: Crazy/Insane/Nuts (as expressions)
Say: Unbelievable, over the top
Why: Offensive to people with psychosocial disabilities.

Instead of: Crazy/Insane/Nuts/Psycho
Say: Person with a psychosocial disability
Why: Offensive to people with psychosocial disabilities.

Instead of: Differently abled
Say: Person with a disability
Why: Inaccurate identification for persons with disabilities as we are all differently abled and have different skillsets, strengths etc.

Instead of: Disabled person
Say: Person with a disability
Why: Does not prioritise the humanity of the person over the disability/an identity trait.

Instead of: First World versus Third World
Say: Specific country names
Why: Racist connotations as it is commonly used to refer to global south countries and to imply that 'Third world' signifies a backwardness as compared with others.

Instead of: He/Him/She/Her
Say: Ask the person which pronouns they prefer (Alternatively: 'They', for example)
Why: The person may not identify themselves using these pronouns.

Instead of: Illegal immigrant/Alien (Used outside of legal context)
Say: Undocumented person / undocumented immigrant
Why: The term is considered to be demeaning and primarily aimed at targeted marginalised groups.

Instead of: Ladies and gentlemen
Say: Colleagues/Team/People
Why: Does not recognise that there may be people in the audience who do not identify within those categories.

Instead of: Lame/Paralytic
Say: Person with a disability
Why: Offensive term to people with mobility related disabilities.

Instead of: Man up
Say: Be brave
Why: Implies that being courageous and strong is a trait associated primarily with masculinity.

Instead of: Mentally disabled
Say: Person with a mental disorder
Why: Emphasises people's humanity by placing the person before the disability – disabilities do not define who a person is.

Instead of: Pow Wow
Say: Quick meeting, chat, etc.
Why: Using this phrase which is associated with an Indigenous Peoples ceremony to refer to a quick business meeting denigrates the long, cultural significance of Pow Wows.

Instead of: Racial Descriptors (e.g., Black, Latino, Indian, Asian, etc.)
Say: Consider the necessity of using race within your text or speech.
Why: —

Instead of: Sexual preference
Say: Sexual orientation
Why: The term implies that sexuality is a choice.

Instead of: Suffering from
Say: Living with / Being treated for
Why: May suggest powerlessness and a poorer quality of life.

Instead of: Tone-deaf
Say: Ignore / be insensitive to
Why: Can be insensitive towards people with hearing impairments.

Instead of: Turn a blind eye
Say: Ignore / be insensitive to
Why: Can be insensitive towards people with blindness.

Instead of: Wheelchair bound
Say: Person in a wheelchair / Person who uses a wheelchair
Why: Does not prioritise the humanity of the person over the disability/an identity trait.

Instead of: White collar/Blue collar
Say: Frontline workers/Support office; Operator/Office worker
Why: May be considered classist. Such terms may impact whether employees feel valued.
