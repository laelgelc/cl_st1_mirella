Implicit bias is an unconscious and automatic thought process that affects behaviors and decisions that can lead to discrimination. The Harvard Implicit Association Test (IAT) is a free online test that can help nurses become aware of how strongly they may associate select characteristics with stereotypes and evaluations of students and colleagues.

Person-First Language
With the use of person-first language, focus is placed on the individual rather than their disability or chronic condition.

Cultural competence involves understanding an individual within the context of their predominant cultural background. When a nurse embodies cultural humility, they dedicate themselves to an ongoing journey of recognizing their personal biases, continuously self-evaluating, and establishing a collaborative relationship with the student, regardless of their own beliefs.

Calling In
The concept of calling in creates a space for individuals who have experienced bias or discrimination to recognize and share with others the harmful impact of such experiences.
Calling in fosters awareness in a compassionate manner, addressing bias, discrimination, or hurtful language within an individual or a small group, whereas calling out directs public focus to a matter when attempts to call in someone have been unsuccessful.
